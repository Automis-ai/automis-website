## How much does AI automation cost for a dental clinic?

AI automation for a dental clinic starts from EUR 297 per month, and that is a flat, predictable fee rather than a meter that spikes when your phone gets busy. You are not billed per call or per minute, so a heavy Monday morning or a post-campaign rush costs you the same as a quiet afternoon. For a practice that lives on booked chairs, predictable pricing matters as much as the headline figure, because your phone traffic is lumpy and per-minute vendors profit exactly when your patients are waiting on hold.

The reason clinics start at this price rather than commissioning a big custom build is that automation works best one workflow at a time. You do not need to automate everything on day one. You need to automate the thing that is losing you the most money first, prove it pays, then decide what comes next. That is what the from-297-EUR jumpstart audit is built to do.

### Why the price is a starting point, not a ceiling

The 297 EUR figure is where a focused clinic deployment begins, not a cap on what is possible. A single-line practice that wants its missed calls answered and its bookings synced sits comfortably at the entry price. A busier multi-site group that wants voice, WhatsApp reminders, and no-show recovery all running together will sit higher, because the agent is doing more work across more lines. The pricing tracks the scope of the job, not hidden usage.

## What gets automated first in a dental clinic?

The missed-call and booking workflow almost always comes first, because unanswered calls are the biggest silent leak in a dental practice. A patient with a toothache who hits your voicemail does not usually leave a message and wait. They dial the next clinic on the list. That call is a new-patient value walking out the door, and it never shows up on your books as a loss because it never became an invoice.

A voice agent picks up every one of those calls, day or night, answers routine questions, checks a live calendar, and books the correct appointment type straight into your system. If a call still slips through, the recovery layer follows up by message instead of letting the lead evaporate. The [Automis Voice AI receptionist](/voice-ai) is built to complete the booking, not just log that the phone rang.

### No-show recovery usually comes second

Once the front door is answered, the next expensive gap is the empty chair. No-shows waste a clinical slot that cannot be resold at short notice, and a manual reminder call is easy to forget on a busy day. Automated reminders across voice and WhatsApp confirm the appointment, offer an easy way to rebook, and fill gaps from a waitlist when someone cancels. Clinics often see this as the second workflow precisely because the cost of a missed slot is so easy to measure.

### A simple sequence most clinics follow

1. Automate missed-call answering and booking first, because it captures new patients you are losing now.
2. Add no-show reminders and rebooking second, to protect the slots you already have.
3. Layer in WhatsApp follow-up and review requests once the first two are paying for themselves.

Keeping to this order means the automation funds the next step out of the revenue it recovers, so you are never paying for capability you have not yet proven.

## What is the real cost of the calls and no-shows you already lose?

The most expensive receptionist is the one you do not have, because every unanswered call and every empty chair is revenue you never invoiced. In a dental practice, a single new-patient case can be worth several hundred euros once you count the first visit and the treatment that follows, so even a handful of missed calls a week adds up fast.

### A worked example on clinic numbers

Take a simple, illustrative example. Say a new patient is worth EUR 300 to your clinic across the first visit and early follow-up, and you miss ten calls a week when the line is busy or closed. If just three of those ten would have booked, that is EUR 900 a week, or roughly EUR 3,600 a month, in bookings that never happened. Against a EUR 297 plan, the automation only has to recover a little over one of those calls a week to pay for itself, and everything above that line is margin you were previously handing to the clinic down the road that did pick up.

No-shows stack on top. Every empty slot is a fixed clinical cost with no revenue against it, and unlike a missed call it is a patient who already agreed to come in. Recovering even one or two no-shows a week with automated reminders often covers the plan on its own.
