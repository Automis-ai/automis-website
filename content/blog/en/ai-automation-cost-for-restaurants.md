## How much does AI automation cost for a restaurant?

AI automation for a restaurant starts from EUR 297 per month, and that is a flat, predictable fee rather than a meter that spikes when your phone gets busy. You are not billed per call or per minute, so a full Friday-night reservation wave costs you the same as a slow midweek afternoon. For a venue that lives on booked covers, predictable pricing matters as much as the headline figure, because restaurant phone traffic is brutally lumpy and per-minute vendors earn most exactly when your guests are stuck on hold.

The reason restaurants start at this price rather than commissioning a big custom build is that automation works best one workflow at a time. You do not need to automate the whole front of house on day one. You need to automate the thing losing you the most money first, prove it pays, then decide what comes next. That is what the from-297-EUR jumpstart audit is built to do.

### Why the price is a starting point, not a ceiling

The 297 EUR figure is where a focused restaurant deployment begins, not a cap on what is possible. A single venue that wants its missed reservation calls answered and its bookings synced sits comfortably at the entry price. A small group running voice, WhatsApp confirmations, and no-show recovery across several locations will sit higher, because the agent is doing more work across more lines. The pricing tracks the scope of the job, not hidden usage, so your bill does not jump because you had a good weekend.

## What gets automated first in a restaurant?

The missed-call and reservation workflow almost always comes first, because unanswered calls during service are the biggest silent leak in a restaurant. A guest deciding where to eat tonight who hits your voicemail does not leave a message and wait. They dial the next place on the list. That call is a table for four walking out the door, and it never shows up in your books as a loss because it never became a bill.

A voice agent picks up every one of those calls, day or night, answers routine questions like opening hours and whether you take large groups, checks live availability, and books the table straight into your system. If a call still slips through at peak, the [missed call text-back safety net](/blog/missed-call-text-back-for-restaurants) follows up by message instead of letting the guest evaporate. The [Automis Voice AI receptionist](/voice-ai) is built to complete the booking, not just log that the phone rang.

### No-show recovery usually comes second

Once the phone is answered, the next expensive gap is the table that was booked and never sat. A no-show wastes a slot you turned others away for, and a manual confirmation call is easy to forget on a service night. Automated reminders across voice and WhatsApp confirm the booking, offer an easy way to cancel or move it, and free the table early enough to resell when someone drops out. Owners often add this second precisely because the cost of an empty booked table is so easy to measure.

### A simple sequence most restaurants follow

1. Automate missed-call answering and reservations first, because it captures covers you are losing right now.
2. Add no-show reminders and easy rebooking second, to protect the tables you already have on the book.
3. Layer in WhatsApp confirmations and review requests once the first two are paying for themselves.

Keeping to this order means the automation funds the next step out of the revenue it recovers, so you are never paying for capability you have not yet proven.

AI automation wins on predictable cost and round-the-clock coverage, hiring wins on human warmth for the guests already at the table, and doing nothing quietly costs the most of all. AI automation gives you predictable pricing and full coverage, and it turns calls that used to ring out into booked tables. This is not an argument to cut your team. It frees them from the phone so they can look after the guest in front of them.

## What is the real cost of the calls and no-shows you already lose?

The most expensive host is the one you do not have, because every unanswered call and every empty booked table is revenue you never invoiced. In a restaurant, a single table can be worth EUR 50 to well over EUR 100 once you count drinks and dessert, so even a handful of missed calls at peak adds up fast.

### A worked example on restaurant numbers

Take a simple, illustrative example, not a promise about your venue. Say an average table is worth EUR 60 to your restaurant, and you miss ten calls a week when the floor is slammed or the doors are shut. If just three of those ten would have booked, that is EUR 180 a week, or roughly EUR 720 a month, in covers that never happened. Against a EUR 297 plan, the automation only has to recover a little over one of those calls a week to pay for itself, and everything above that line is margin you were previously handing to the venue down the street that did pick up.

No-shows stack on top. Every booked table that never sits is a slot you may have turned a walk-in away for, and unlike a missed call it is a guest who already agreed to come in. Recovering even one or two no-shows a week with automated reminders often covers the plan on its own.
