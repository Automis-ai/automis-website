The cheapest phone answering service for a small business is a per-call or per-message human service while you take a few dozen calls a month. Once you pass a few hundred calls, or you want every caller booked into your diary rather than just logged, a flat-fee AI voice assistant is usually the cheaper option. ## What does each type of answering service cost?

Human answering services charge by the call, the message or the minute, and AI voice assistants charge a flat monthly fee. That one difference decides almost everything about which is cheaper for you.

Here is what I could confirm from vendor pages while writing this.

Connect Communications, a UK human service, publishes one price: [entry-level packages from 14.99 GBP a month plus VAT](https://www.connect-communications.co.uk/cost-calculator/call-answering-service-costs/). Its standard tariffs are based on inclusive monthly messages, but the page gives no number. It says it does not charge for wrong numbers, spam calls or obvious sales calls. Mid-level and diary management tiers exist, with no published price.

Moneypenny, the best-known UK name, lists telephone answering, appointment booking and out-of-hours cover on its [pricing page](https://www.moneypenny.com/uk/pricing/) but shows no prices. It is price on request, so I cannot put a number on it.

Ruby, a US service, does publish plans: [50 minutes for 250 USD a month, 100 minutes for 395 USD, 200 minutes for 720 USD and 500 minutes for 1,725 USD](https://www.ruby.com/pricing/), with no overage rate listed. I include it only to show what per-minute pricing looks like at scale. It is a US business, so it may not suit a UK or EU practice.

## When is a per-call answering service the cheapest?

A per-call service is the cheapest when you get few calls and mostly need a message taken. If you receive 20 calls a month and nobody needs to book anything on the phone, an entry package at around 15 GBP a month is hard to beat, and I would not try to talk you out of it.

The same goes for a sole trader who only wants out-of-hours cover. A person takes a message, emails it to you, and you ring back in the morning. A 297 EUR plan would be overkill for that.

The trouble starts when you ask the service to do more than take messages.

## How do the costs change as your call volume grows?

Per-call costs grow in a straight line with volume, while a flat fee stays put, so there is always a crossover point. Where it sits depends on your real per-call rate, which you should get in writing.

Here is a worked example, and it is an illustration with invented rates, not a quote from any vendor. Say a human service charges 1.50 GBP per call with a 5 GBP monthly minimum. Then:

| Calls per month | Illustrative human cost per month | Flat AI fee (297 EUR) |
|---|---|---|
| 30 | about 45 GBP | 297 EUR |
| 100 | about 150 GBP | 297 EUR |
| 250 | about 375 GBP | 297 EUR |
| 500 | about 750 GBP | 297 EUR |

On those made-up rates, the two cross somewhere around 200 to 250 calls a month. Change the per-call rate and the crossover moves. At a higher per-minute rate, or when calls run long, it moves down fast. That is why I would never trust a headline "from" price. Ask what counts as a billable call, what happens on long calls, and what the diary-booking tier costs on top.

Not all calls are equal either. A message-taking call is short. A booking call, where the caller picks a time, gets moved once and asks about parking, is not. Per-minute plans punish exactly the calls you want.

## What does a missed call actually cost a clinic?

A missed call costs the value of the appointment that went to someone else, which is usually far more than the answering service itself. This is the number that should drive your decision, more than the monthly fee.

When we built the AI receptionist for Clinica Santa Maria, a dental clinic, the problem was missed calls, booking and no-shows, not the cost of a message service. A message tells the patient someone will call back. A booking assistant lets them book while they are still on the line, and sends the reminder that cuts the no-show.

I will not quote you a recovery figure I cannot source. Work out your own with our [free missed call revenue calculator](/tools/missed-call-revenue-calculator): put in your calls, your miss rate and your average appointment value. If the number it gives is under 297 EUR a month, a cheap message service is probably the right call. If it is several times that, the fee matters much less than what the phone is losing.

## How do they differ in a real working day?

In a working day, the human service takes a message and you chase it, while the AI assistant books the appointment on the call. Take a small practice that is busy from 9 to 12 and has no receptionist on the desk.

At 9:40 a new patient rings. With a per-message human service, a polite agent takes the name and number, and an email arrives. You call back at lunch. By then the patient has often tried the next practice, which picked up. With an AI voice assistant connected to your calendar, the caller hears available slots, picks one and gets a confirmation by WhatsApp or SMS, and the booking is already in your diary when you finish your 9:30 patient.

At 6:30 in the evening, both do their job. At 2 am, both do their job. The difference is what happens after the call ends.

## What are the strengths and limits of each?

Human services win on empathy and on odd conversations, and AI wins on price at volume and on getting things done during the call.

A person is better when the caller is upset, confused or has a question nobody planned for. A human service is also simpler to buy: sign up, forward the line, done. The limits are cost that rises with each call, messages instead of bookings on the cheap tiers, and prices that many vendors keep hidden.

An AI assistant is better when your calls are repetitive: booking, rescheduling, opening hours, prices, directions. It does not tire, runs in more than one language and never puts a caller on hold. The limit is real: it is not for every conversation, and ours is not a self-serve app you sign up for and configure in an afternoon. ## Which one should you choose?

Choose by call volume, by what you need done on the call, and by how much one lost booking costs you.

### Choose a per-call human service if

- You take fewer than about 50 calls a month.
- You only want messages taken, mostly out of hours.
- A callback the next morning is acceptable in your trade.
- You value a human voice more than a booked slot.

### Choose a flat-fee AI voice assistant if

- You take a few hundred calls a month, or expect to.
- Most calls are bookings, rescheduling or the same five questions.
- You lose appointments when nobody answers, and your calendar is the thing that needs filling.
- You want predictable monthly cost with no per-minute surprises.
