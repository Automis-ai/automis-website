## Why look beyond Smith.ai?

Three things matter most when you compare: price, currency and fit. Smith.ai's [published plans](https://smith.ai/pricing) are Starter at USD 300 a month for 30 calls, Basic at USD 810 for 90 calls and Pro at USD 2,100 for 300 calls. Extra calls cost between USD 8.50 and USD 11.50 each. Appointment booking is an add-on at USD 1.50 a call, and a Spanish line is another USD 1.00 a call.

That is a fair price for a live agent in the US. For a UK or EU practice it raises three questions. Do you want to pay in dollars? Does a service that lists only English and Spanish cover your callers? And does the page say it serves you at all? It doesn't mention customers outside the US, so I would not assume it does.

## How did I pick these five?

I looked for options a UK or EU business can actually buy, with prices you can verify. I weighed four things:

- Price per call or per month, and what is billed on top.
- Languages, because callers in Europe often switch mid-sentence.
- Booking into a real calendar, not just taking a message.
- Contract length, since a rolling month is much easier to leave.

I dropped vendors whose pages did not tell me where they sell, because I could not confirm they serve UK or EU callers.

## 2. Moneypenny

Moneypenny is the best known UK name, with live receptionists and an [AI receptionist](https://www.moneypenny.com/uk/ai-receptionist). The AI page says it can book appointments and names HubSpot, Salesforce and Calendly as integrations, plus "many more". It describes voices by accent, such as a Yorkshire accent.

Pricing is built around you. Its [answering service page](https://www.moneypenny.com/uk/telephone-answering-service) shows no prices and asks you to request a quote. Contracts for smaller schemes (150 minutes or fewer a month) have a three-month minimum, and larger ones run six or twelve months.

- Best for: firms that want an established UK brand and the option of a human voice.
- Price: price on request.
- Why it stands out: both human and AI answering from one UK provider.
- Worth knowing: no published prices, a minimum term on the live service, and the pages I read list no languages beyond English (en-GB).

## 3. Connect Communications

Connect Communications is a UK message-taking service with [published tariffs](https://www.connect-communications.co.uk/?p=8710). It starts at 14.99 GBP a month for 5 messages, then 29.99 GBP for 15, 54.99 GBP for 30 and 153.99 GBP for 100, all excluding VAT. Billing is per message, and it runs on a rolling monthly contract with no setup fee and a 30-day free trial.

This is the cheapest route on the list, and for a plumber or a sole trader who only needs a name and a number, it is hard to beat. It is not an AI receptionist, and the page describes diary entries as a separate service.

- Best for: sole traders and micro businesses that want calls answered and messages passed on.
- Price: from 14.99 GBP a month (ex VAT).
- Why it stands out: clear tariff, rolling monthly contract, no setup fee.
- Worth knowing: the tariff page was last updated in July 2020, so confirm today's prices, and it lists no languages or booking.

## 4. Ringly

Ringly is a Dutch company (the site gives a Rotterdam address) selling an AI phone assistant. Its [pricing](https://www.ringly.io/pricing) is simple: USD 99 a month including your first 50 conversations, then USD 1.99 each, or pay-as-you-go at USD 1.99 a conversation. Call length does not change the price. It supports 40 languages and can switch between them mid-call. There is no contract, and a 30-day money-back guarantee.

The pages I read name Gorgias and Zendesk, and say it connects to "any tool with an API". They do not name calendar tools or list supported countries, so test your calendar and your UK or EU number before paying.

- Best for: multilingual businesses wanting a low-cost, self-serve AI.
- Price: USD 99 a month with 50 conversations included.
- Why it stands out: 40 languages and a credit if it resolves under 65% of calls in a month.
- Worth knowing: calendar integrations and supported countries are not stated on the pages I read, and it is billed in dollars.

## 5. Dialzara

Dialzara sells AI receptionist plans from its [pricing page](https://dialzara.com/pricing): Business Lite at USD 29 for 60 minutes, Pro at USD 99 for 220, Plus at USD 199 for 500 and Elite at USD 349 for 1,000. Overage runs from USD 0.35 to USD 0.48 a minute. There is a 7-day free trial.

The Lite plan includes English and Spanish and a CRM or calendar integration, but the page does not name calendar providers or the countries it serves.

- Best for: a small business that wants to try an AI receptionist cheaply and configure it alone.
- Price: from USD 29 a month for 60 minutes.
- Why it stands out: the lowest AI entry price I found, with a free trial.
- Worth knowing: billed per minute, so long calls cost more, and no UK or EU coverage is stated.

## How should you choose?

The right choice depends on whether a call must end in a booking. Here is how I would split it.

- Choose Automis if someone else should build and maintain it.
- Choose Moneypenny if you want a known UK brand and are happy to get a quote.
- Choose Connect Communications if you only need messages taken.
- Choose Ringly if languages matter most and you will do the setup yourself.
- Choose Dialzara if you want to test AI answering for under USD 30.
- Stay with Smith.ai if your callers are in the US and you want live US agents.

Whichever you pick, ask for one test booking into your own calendar before you sign. That single call shows you more than any pricing page. For a wider view of what AI answering costs, see our [AI receptionist cost guide](/blog/ai-receptionist-cost).

## Should you pick an AI receptionist or a human answering service?

Pick an AI receptionist if most of your calls end in a booking or a routine question, and a human service if many calls need judgement, empathy or a named person. The two are not exclusive. Moneypenny sells both a live service and an AI receptionist from one UK provider, so you can start with one and add the other.

Picture a dental front desk at 6pm with 3 missed calls: two people wanting an appointment and one worried about pain after treatment. An AI receptionist can book the first two into the calendar on the spot, while a human service is the better fit for the third, because judgement and reassurance matter there. This is an illustration, not a client result.

The trade-off is in how each is billed. A per-message service costs more with every call it takes. The AI options here bill per conversation or per minute, or by monthly plan, and none of the pages I read describe a human stepping in on a hard call. If your calls are mixed, route the routine ones to AI and keep a person for the rest, and check how each vendor hands a call over before you sign.
